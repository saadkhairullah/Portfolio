Overview

A live map of 350+ abandoned underground coal mines in Texas, showing the boundaries they actually mined inside, so builders and buyers know what is under a plot before they build on it.

Where the idea came from

I watched a Practical Engineering video about old underground coal mines, some of them 50 to 200 years old, causing the ground above them to sink. They were never maintained and mostly never documented properly, and water pressure builds up in the ground above them until it gives out.

I wanted to know whether Texas had any. The Railroad Commission of Texas has records, but the only map they publish is a PDF, and it is hard to read and impossible to search. What they do publish is the underlying GeoJSON dataset. After digging into it I realized I could visualize that data far better than their own map did.

What we built

I led a team of 5 to build it as a class project: assigning work, running standups and sprints, and making the architecture calls. Next.js and TypeScript on the front, MongoDB behind it, MapboxGL rendering the GeoJSON.

The result clearly shows each mine and the boundary it operated within. It is meant for home builders and business owners who need to know whether the land they are about to build on sits over one of them.
